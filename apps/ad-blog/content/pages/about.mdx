ToolProof publishes practical guides to AI and everyday tech tools: how to use a new feature, how to fix an error that stops you, and how to choose between tools that look alike.

Most "news" about a new AI feature tells you that it launched. We focus on what comes next: where the setting actually is, what the free plan really includes, what to do when it doesn't work, and whether it is worth switching.

## What we cover

- **AI settings and privacy**: how to turn off or limit the AI features built into Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Gmail, Word, Windows, iPhone, and Instagram, and how to check whether an image was made with AI
- **Free photo, video, and PDF tools**: converting, compressing, splitting, and cleaning up files with free apps, measured side by side on the same test files
- **Offline AI tools**: free apps that read text from images or transcribe audio on your own computer, without uploading your files
- **News & Analysis**: changes to Windows, Microsoft 365, phones, and subscriptions that affect what you pay or what still works, with the steps to take

## How we check our guides

- **We run it ourselves where we can.** If an app, setting, or website can be tested directly, we test it on a current version and write down exactly what happened, including the steps that did not work.
- **We cite primary sources.** Feature details, plan limits, and prices come from official documentation, release notes, and pricing pages, and we link to them so you can check.
- **We say what we could not test.** Some features are limited to a paid plan, a region, or a specific device. When a step comes from documentation rather than our own test, the article says so.
- **We update instead of guessing.** AI tools change quickly. When a feature, price, or menu changes, we update the guide and its "Updated" date.

The full process, including how we choose topics and keep facts and opinion apart, is in our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy).
