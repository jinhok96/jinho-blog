If you're signed in to more than one Google account on an Android phone, your phone number has probably been added to all of them. Google's Android help page says phone number verification is on by default and adds the phone's verified numbers to every Google account signed in on the device, including accounts and numbers you add later. You can turn it off in your phone's Settings app, but that only stops new links. Removing the number from accounts that already have it is a separate step, one account at a time.

On October 24, 2026, we read Google's Android help page on phone number verification and three Google Account help pages on phone numbers, sign-in and recovery, and compared what they say. They don't describe the linking the same way, and one of them documents a per-number switch that lets your phone number be used for ads. We don't have an Android phone or a Google account in our test setup, so the steps below come from Google's help pages, not our own screens.

## What happened

Android Authority reported on October 14, 2026 that Android users were getting emails saying their phone number had been verified, then finding that number on secondary, work and throwaway Google accounts on the same phone. Android Central, BGR, Malwarebytes, Lifehacker and, on October 24, Engadget followed.

The behavior is written in Google's own help page, [Manage phone number verification on your device](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7521240?hl=en): "On your device, phone number verification is turned on by default. We verify the phone number and add them to all Google Accounts that are signed in on the device. This applies to both current and future accounts and phone numbers." Google's example: a new account links to every number on the phone, and a new number is added to every account.

![Google's Android help page section "Turn phone number verification on or off," with a box around the text saying verification is on by default and verified numbers are added to all Google Accounts signed in on the device, for current and future accounts and numbers](./images/google-phone-number-verification/google-help-default-all-accounts.webp)

The default isn't new this month. Android Authority reported that Google added this wording to the help page between August and October 2025, and Kaspersky wrote in July 2026 that verification became widely visible after a September 2025 Google system update. As of October 24, 2026, we found no public statement from Google about the reports. Android Authority said it had asked Google for comment.

![Timeline: in late 2025 the help page gained on-by-default and all-accounts wording, according to Android Authority; on October 14, 2026 Android Authority reported the linking; from October 18 to 22 other outlets followed; on October 24 Engadget reported it while Google's page still said on by default, with no public Google statement](./images/google-phone-number-verification/phone-number-linking-timeline.webp)

## Who is affected

The setting applies to Android phones only. Google's page sits in Android Help and says phone number verification can only be managed in the device's settings.

| Your setup | What Google's help page means for you |
| --- | --- |
| One Google account on the phone | The phone's number is verified and added to that one account |
| A second personal account, such as a separate Gmail address | The same number is added to every account signed in, including ones you kept apart on purpose |
| A work or school account on your personal phone | Google's page doesn't exclude them, and Android Authority reported work accounts getting the number |
| Two SIMs or an eSIM plus a SIM | Each verified number is added to each account |
| Someone else's account signed in on your phone | Google's wording covers "all Google Accounts that are signed in on the device," not only your own |
| iPhone | Not covered: Google's page describes Android devices |

## What changes for you

**Your accounts are no longer separated by phone number.** Google's own page on [signing in with your phone number](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/7222352?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid) tells you to use a number that "Belongs only to you" and that "You don't use on another Google Account to sign in." Automatic verification puts that one number on every account signed in on the phone.

![Three Google help pages compared: Android Help says verified numbers are added to all Google Accounts signed in on the device; Google Account Help says they can be added to any account signed in; the sign-in help page says to use a number that belongs only to you and that you don't use on another Google Account to sign in](./images/google-phone-number-verification/google-help-pages-compared.webp)

**Google's pages don't describe the linking the same way.** The Android page says numbers are added to all accounts. Google Account Help on [how phone numbers are used](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3463280?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid) says verified numbers "can be added to any Google Account" on the device, and describes giving Google your number when you set up an account as a choice: "you can let Google know your phone's number."

**Your number can be used for ads.** Each phone number on a Google account has a **Better ads and Google services** setting. Google says it "lets your phone number to be used across Google services to show you ads more relevant to you," and when it's on, **Across Google** appears next to **Usage** for that number. Google's pages don't say whether numbers added by automatic verification start with this setting on, so check each account.

**A replacement phone can find all your accounts.** Google's sign-in page says that when you set up a new Android phone, Google will "attempt to identify the Google Accounts associated with the phone number" so you can sign in without typing your email. With one number on every account, that list would likely include all of them, although Google still asks for approval from another signed-in device or your old phone's screen lock.

**Verification keeps running.** Google and your carrier "will try to periodically reverify" your numbers, and "Carrier charges may apply."

## What to do now

If you use only one Google account on your phone, you can leave verification on. Google's page says it helps you sign in with your number, get back into your account, use emergency location, find or lock a lost phone, receive RCS chats, and use Quick Share.

If you keep accounts apart, follow these steps:

1. Open your phone's **Settings** app and tap **Google > All services**. On some phones the Google entry has a longer name, such as **Google services and preferences**, Android Authority reported.
2. Under **Privacy & security**, tap **Phone number verification** and turn automatic verification off. Android Authority reported the switch is labeled **Verify phone numbers automatically**.
3. Go back to **Settings > Google**, select the account that shouldn't have your number at the top, then tap **Manage your Google Account > Personal info > Phone**.
4. Before you remove the number, open **Security & sign-in** and make sure the account has a recovery email. Google's [recovery options page](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/183723?hl=en) says recovery info is how you get back in if you forget your password or get locked out.
5. Back on the **Phone** page, select the number and tap **Delete > Remove number**. Repeat steps 3 to 5 for every account where you don't want it.
6. For accounts that keep the number, open the same **Phone** page, select the number and turn off **Better ads and Google services** if you don't want it used for ads.

![Three places to check: the phone setting stops future verification but leaves numbers already added; removing the number from each Google Account's Phone page takes it off that account but also removes it for sign-in and recovery unless you add it back as a recovery phone; turning off Better ads and Google services stops ad use while keeping the number for sign-in and recovery](./images/google-phone-number-verification/phone-number-switches.webp)

Google also documents a middle option: remove the number, then add it back only as a **Recovery phone** under **Security & sign-in**. That stops the "Across Google" use but keeps the account tied to your number. If the number won't come off an account, our guide to [removing a phone number from your Google account](/posts/remove-phone-number-from-google-account) covers the recovery phone, 2-Step Verification and the other places it can stay.

Android Authority reported that some Reddit users saw the verification switch turn itself back on, though Android Authority couldn't reproduce it. Check the setting again after a system update.

The phone number isn't the only Google default worth checking. Our look at [Google's privacy policy change on Gmail and Photos ads](/posts/google-gmail-photos-ads) covers a separate switch that keeps your inbox out of ads, and our [guide to turning off AI in Chrome, Gmail, Word and more](/posts/turn-off-ai-features) shows where Google's AI settings live.

## Our take

If you keep more than one Google account on your Android phone, turn automatic verification off and remove the number from every account that shouldn't have it. Google should make this a choice you make for each account, at the moment you add it.

The facts behind that: a second account usually exists to stay apart from the main one. Google's own sign-in help tells you not to reuse a sign-in number across accounts, yet its Android default puts one number on every account without asking, including accounts added later. Turning the switch off doesn't undo past links, so the cleanup falls on you, account by account.

The other side: a verified number is how many people get back into an account after losing a password, and Google ties RCS chats, Quick Share and emergency location to it. For the many people with one account, the default is likely a net help. Google's page also lists what verification supports but not what stops working when you turn it off, so switching it off on a single-account phone could cost features Google doesn't spell out.
