Apple's iOS 27.2 update, released October 26, 2026, lets Siri AI recall your past conversations and pull answers from photos attached in Messages, Calendar event attendees and your voicemails. It only reaches people who turned on Siri AI (Beta) on an iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max or an iPhone 16 model or later. Apple's guide lists no switch that turns off the recall on its own: you limit it by deleting Siri conversations or shortening **Settings > Siri > Keep Conversations**, and you keep an app's data away from Siri with the new **Settings > Siri > Excluded Apps** list, which also removes that app from Search and suggestions.

On October 26 (UTC), we compared Apple's iOS 27.2 release notes with five iPhone User Guide pages, two Apple privacy pages and three security notes, and checked them against the copy of the guide we saved on October 23 for [our guide to turning off Apple Intelligence on iPhone](/posts/turn-off-apple-intelligence-iphone). Apple's own pages don't agree yet: the updated guide sends you to Excluded Apps, while Apple's Siri privacy page, dated September 14, still describes a switch inside each app's Siri settings.

| What changed in iOS 27.2 | Who gets it | Where you control it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Siri AI recalls past conversations | Siri AI users on iPhone 15 Pro and later | Siri app history, **Keep Conversations** |
| Siri AI uses Messages photos, Calendar attendees and voicemails | Siri AI users on iPhone 15 Pro and later | **Excluded Apps**: Messages, Calendar, Phone |
| One list of apps hidden from Siri, Search and suggestions | Listed with the Siri AI changes | **Settings > Siri > Excluded Apps** |
| 163 security fixes | iPhone 11 and later | **Settings > General > Software Update** |

## What happened

Apple released iOS 27.2 for the iPhone 11 and later on October 26, 2026. Its [release notes](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149076) say the update gives Siri AI "the ability to retain useful context from previous Siri interactions as well as draw from a wider range of personal context." Under the Siri AI heading, for all iPhone 16 models and later, iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 15 Pro Max, Apple lists these changes:

- Siri AI "can recall your past conversations, so you can ask Siri about something it told you before, like the restaurants it recommended last week."
- Siri AI can draw on "photos attached in Messages, Calendar event attendees, your voicemails, and better results from Safari history and Reading List."
- Excluded Apps "lets you choose which apps Siri, Search, and suggestions can access, all from one setting."
- You can select and copy part of a Siri response.
- Siri AI now works in French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish, and still isn't available in the EU or mainland China ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/siri-ai-is-available-in-more-languages-starting-today/)).

The same day, Apple released iOS 26.7.2 for iPhones that stay on iOS 26 and iOS 18.7.11 for the iPhone XS, XS Max and XR ([Apple security releases](https://support.apple.com/en-us/100100)). Other iOS 27.2 additions include Suggestions in Messages, Apple TV profiles and support for SynthID, the invisible AI watermark we explained in [how to check if an image is AI-generated](/posts/check-if-image-is-ai-generated), in Photos and Image Playground.

## Who is affected

Only iPhones that can run Siri AI get the new memory, and only if you turned Siri AI on. Apple's [Siri AI guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-started-with-siri-ai-iphv6zwrg8jvfgr/ios) says you switch it on yourself with **Settings > Siri > Try Siri AI (Beta)**, and that it isn't available to users under 13.

## What changes for you

Siri AI already searched your photos, mail, messages and calendar to answer questions. In iOS 27.2, it can also look back at your earlier Siri conversations, and it reaches three kinds of data it didn't list before: photos people attach in Messages, who is invited to your Calendar events, and your voicemails. Apple's [Siri app guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/siri-ai-use-the-siri-app-p7ny364ngxlj/ios) says those conversations are saved in the Siri app and sync to your other Apple devices signed in to the same Apple Account.

![Table of what Siri AI can use after iOS 27.2 and the setting that limits each: past Siri conversations are limited by deleting chats or Keep Conversations; photos attached in Messages, Calendar event attendees, voicemails, and Safari history and Reading List are limited by adding Messages, Calendar, Phone or Safari to Excluded Apps, which also removes that app from Search and suggestions](./images/ios-27-2-siri-ai-memory/siri-ai-ios-27-2-data-and-settings.webp)

Excluded Apps works one whole app at a time. Apple's [Siri settings guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-siri-settings-iphc28624b81abc/ios) says "Excluded apps aren't available to Siri, Search, or suggestions," and that all apps are included by default. So adding Messages to keep its photos away from Siri also takes Messages out of Search and suggestions. Because the same guide says app access is what lets Siri "find information and take action in that app," excluding Phone to protect voicemails should also stop Siri from acting in the Phone app. We couldn't test that on an iPhone.

![Apple's iPhone User Guide section Exclude apps from Siri, Search, and suggestions, with a box around the sentences saying excluded apps aren't available to Siri, Search, or suggestions and all apps are included by default, followed by the steps Settings, Siri, Excluded Apps, Add Apps](./images/ios-27-2-siri-ai-memory/apple-guide-excluded-apps.webp)

Apple's guide and release notes don't describe a setting that turns off the recall while keeping Siri AI on, as of October 27, 2026. The controls Apple documents for Siri conversations are deleting them and choosing how long to keep them, and Apple doesn't say whether deleting a conversation also removes what Siri retained from it. Our guide to [seeing and deleting Siri history on iPhone](/posts/siri-history-on-iphone) covers the other places your Siri requests are kept, including Apple's own copy.

Apple's pages also differ on where your requests go. Its [Apple Intelligence privacy guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/apple-intelligence-and-privacy-iphe3f499e0e/ios) says requests sent to Private Cloud Compute, Apple's servers for larger models, are "not stored or made accessible to Apple." Its [Siri, Dictation & Privacy page](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/ask-siri-dictation/), dated September 14, 2026, says Apple stores transcripts of Siri interactions under a random identifier and may keep them for up to two years. Neither page says which rule covers a Siri AI conversation.

## What to do now

You can update first and adjust Siri afterward, or skip Siri AI entirely. Apple's [update guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/128058) and Siri pages give these steps:

1. **Install the update.** Go to **Settings > General > Software Update**. An iPhone XS, XS Max or XR gets iOS 18.7.11 from the same screen, and that one fixes a bug Apple says may have been exploited.
2. **Shorten how long Siri keeps conversations.** Go to **Settings > Siri > Keep Conversations** and choose **30 days** or **1 year**. Apple says older conversations are then permanently deleted.
3. **Delete conversations you don't want Siri to look back on.** In the **Siri** app, touch and hold a conversation and tap **Delete**. To remove several, tap the button in the top-right corner, then **Select**.
4. **Keep an app's data away from Siri.** Go to **Settings > Siri > Excluded Apps > Add Apps**, select Messages, Calendar, Phone or Safari, then tap the checkmark. Those apps also disappear from Search and suggestions.
5. **See what went to Apple's servers.** Go to **Settings > Privacy & Security > Apple Intelligence Report**, choose the last 7 days, then tap **Export Activity**.
6. **Turn Siri AI off.** Go to **Settings > Siri > Turn Off Siri**, then **Turn on Siri > Siri Classic**. Our guide to [turning off Apple Intelligence on iPhone](/posts/turn-off-apple-intelligence-iphone) covers the other features and the Screen Time lock.

## Our take

Siri AI's memory needs its own off switch and a clear answer on deletion before it leaves beta. Apple is adding recall and three new data sources in one update, but the only documented ways to limit recall are deleting conversations or shortening their storage, and the only way to wall off an app also removes it from Search. Google's Gemini, by comparison, has a separate **Memory** switch and lets you ask "Did you use any info from past chats?" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16598469)), as we covered in [our guide to turning off Gemini Apps Activity](/posts/turn-off-gemini-apps-activity). Apple should also update its Siri privacy page, which still points to a per-app switch and doesn't say how long Siri AI conversations are kept on its side.

The other view: Siri AI is opt-in, much of it runs on the iPhone or on servers Apple says don't store requests, and one Excluded Apps list is easier to manage than a switch inside every app. For most people, setting Keep Conversations to 30 days and excluding Phone if voicemails are sensitive is a reasonable middle ground.
