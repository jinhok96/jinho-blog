Spotify can now ask a Premium Family member to prove they live with the plan manager by scanning a QR code on the manager's phone, in person, within 7 days. If the member misses that window or can't complete the scan, Spotify says they can't join a different Family or Duo plan for 12 months. For a household that really lives together, this is one quick scan. For a family with someone living elsewhere, such as a college student or a grown child who moved out, it can mean paying $12.99 a month for Individual instead of a share of the $21.99 Family plan.

The thing to do now is make sure the plan manager knows where the QR code is, and to sort out anyone on the plan who doesn't live at the plan address before Spotify asks.

We read Spotify's support pages for the Family and Duo plans, its Paid Subscription Terms and its U.S. Premium price page on October 19, 2026.

## What happened

Spotify's support article [Address and verification for Family plan](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/family-address-verification/) now has a "QR code verification" section. It says Spotify "may ask some Premium Family members to verify they live with the plan manager by scanning a QR code." The manager opens the code in the Spotify mobile app, the member scans it with their phone camera, and "both the manager and member need to be in the same place." Members get 7 days from the notification. 9to5Google [reported on October 19, 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/10/19/spotify-qr-code-verification-family-sharing/) that Reddit users had started seeing the prompt.

The household rule itself isn't new. Spotify's [Paid Subscription Terms](https://www.spotify.com/us/legal/premium-family-terms/) say everyone on a Family or Duo plan must be "family members residing at the same address," that Spotify may ask for re-verification of the home address, and that it can end access if you don't meet those rules. Until now, the check Spotify described was typing in the full address. The QR scan adds a check that a typed address can't pass.

## Who is affected

| If you are... | What the QR check means for you |
| --- | --- |
| A Family plan manager | You don't scan anything, but members need you in person to show them the code |
| A Family member living with the manager | One scan within 7 days of the prompt, if Spotify asks you |
| A Family member living elsewhere (college, moved out, another relative's home) | You can't complete the scan, and a miss blocks you from other Family and Duo plans for 12 months |
| On a Duo plan | Spotify's [Duo verification page](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/address-verification-duo/) still describes only the address check, with the same 7-day window and 12-month block |
| On Individual, Student or Free | No change |

Spotify says it asks "some" Family members, not all, and it doesn't say how it picks them. Its terms exclude kids' and managed accounts from the address check when a plan is set up, and the QR section doesn't mention them.

## What changes for you

The 7-day clock starts when the member gets the notification, and the scan only works in the mobile app with both phones in the same place. Spotify's troubleshooting notes say the code "changes every few seconds," so a photo or screenshot of it won't work.

![Diagram of Spotify's Family plan QR code check: a member has 7 days from the prompt to scan the plan manager's code in person; if they scan in time they stay on Premium Family, and if they miss it or can't scan they lose the Family plan and can't join a different Family or Duo plan for 12 months, but can still buy Individual at $12.99 a month or Student at $6.99 a month](./images/spotify-family-qr-code-verification/qr-verification-timeline.webp)

If the member misses the window, the QR section says they "can't join a different Family or Duo plan for 12 months" and points them to other plans. For the older address check, the same page says a member who fails "loses access to the plan and switches to the free version," but can rejoin the same Family or Duo plan. The QR section doesn't repeat that rejoin wording, so if you fail a QR check we wouldn't count on getting back onto the same plan without living at that address.

The cost of a miss depends on how many people share the plan. As of October 19, 2026, Spotify's [U.S. Premium page](https://www.spotify.com/us/premium/) lists Family at $21.99 a month for up to six accounts, Duo at $18.99 for two, Individual at $12.99 and Student at $6.99 for eligible college students, before tax.

![Bar chart of what each person pays per month on Spotify Premium in the U.S.: Family split six ways is $3.67, four ways $5.50, three ways $7.33, Duo split two ways $9.50, Student $6.99 and Individual $12.99; moving from a shared plan to Individual costs $111.90 a year more from a six-person Family plan, $89.91 from four, $67.92 from three and $41.94 from Duo](./images/spotify-family-qr-code-verification/spotify-cost-per-person.webp)

A member who moves from a full six-person Family plan to Individual pays $111.90 more a year, if the family splits the bill evenly. A college student who qualifies for Student pays $6.99 a month, which is still more than an even share of a Family plan with four or more people.

## What to do now

1. **Update the Spotify app on every phone on the plan.** Spotify says QR verification only works in the mobile app and asks everyone to have the latest version.
2. **Plan manager: find the QR code before anyone needs it.** In the Spotify app, tap your **profile picture**, then **Your Premium**, then **Manage**.
3. **Members: scan as soon as the prompt appears.** Don't wait for the weekend if the manager is home tonight. You need to be in the same room as the manager's phone.
4. **If the scan fails, run Spotify's checks.** Make sure both phones show the correct date and time, close and reopen the app, and try again in person. If it still fails, contact [Spotify support](https://support.spotify.com/us/contact-spotify-support/) within the 7 days.
5. **Move anyone who doesn't live with you before Spotify asks.** A college student can check eligibility for Student at $6.99 a month. Two adults who live together elsewhere can share Duo at $18.99. Spotify's [plan change guide](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/change-premium-plans/) says Family members currently have to cancel to Free first, then sign up for a new plan, and members can switch plans only once every 12 months.

Reports online describe ways around the scan, such as video calls. We aren't covering them: Spotify's terms require a shared address, and getting caught later ends the same way as failing the scan.

## Our take

The rule hasn't changed, but the penalty for a miss is now much harder to avoid. Spotify has always required Family members to live at the same address, and Duo and Family have carried the 12-month block for failed address checks. What the QR scan changes is the proof: an address can be typed from anywhere, while a scan needs two people in one place inside 7 days.

Our view: for households that live together, this is a non-event if you treat the prompt as urgent. The risk is real families that spend part of the year apart, such as students at college, people working away from home, or a member traveling when the prompt arrives. Spotify's page offers troubleshooting but no published way to ask for more time, and a missed week costs a year of access to shared plans. We think Spotify should publish an extension or appeal route before it widens the check. Until it does, the safer move for a family with someone away is to put that person on Student or Individual on your own schedule, rather than waiting for a prompt that may arrive during exams or a trip.

The other side: Spotify says it asks only "some" members, and the price of Family, $21.99 for up to six accounts, depends on most of those accounts belonging to one household. For a family that actually lives together, the new check costs a few seconds once, and the 7-day window is enough for anyone who is home most nights.

## What to watch next
