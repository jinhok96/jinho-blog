There is no single switch that turns off AI everywhere. Each app has its own setting: **Settings > AI innovations** in Chrome, **Manage Workspace smart feature settings** in Gmail, **File > Options > Copilot** in Word, **Settings > Apps > Installed apps** to remove the Copilot app from Windows 11, and a separate switch for each Apple Intelligence feature on an iPhone with iOS 27. Google Search is the exception: Google says AI Overviews can't be turned off, so the closest you can get is the **Web** filter after each search.

Some of these switches remove the AI feature completely, while others only hide a button. To tell them apart, we read Apple, Google and Microsoft's own help pages on October 23, 2026, and combined them with our earlier guides, which checked Chrome's menus against its source code and matched every Gmail and Word AI feature to the setting that controls it. ## Is there one switch that turns off all AI?

No company offers one switch for all of its AI features, and no setting on your phone or computer turns off AI in every app at once. Each product's AI is controlled where that product's other settings live, and turning off one doesn't change the others. Gmail's switch doesn't affect Chrome, and Word's checkbox doesn't affect Excel or Windows.

The words also differ by company. Google groups its switches under "smart features" and "AI innovations," Microsoft under "Copilot" and "connected experiences," and Apple under "Apple Intelligence & Siri." Searching each app's settings for those words is the quickest way to find a switch we don't cover here.

## How to turn off AI in Chrome

Chrome's AI switches are on one page: open the three-dot menu, choose **Settings**, then click **AI innovations**. In Chrome 156 that page lets you turn off **History search, powered by AI**, stop **Help me write** from offering itself, and turn off **On-device AI**, which also deletes the AI model Chrome downloaded to your computer.

Gemini in Chrome and AI Mode in the address bar have no full off switch in Settings. You can hide Gemini's button and shortcut, but removing it completely takes a Chrome policy set with Registry Editor on Windows. Our guide to [turning off AI in Chrome, including the on-device model](/posts/turn-off-ai-in-chrome) lists every switch with the matching policy, checked against Chrome's own source code.

Chrome 156 also changed a security default: it now stops you before opening sites without HTTPS. That change isn't AI, but if you're reviewing Chrome's settings anyway, our look at [Chrome's new warning before insecure HTTP sites](/posts/chrome-http-warning-default) compares its Standard and Enhanced protection options. If you're coming to Chrome from Arc, which stops working on December 22, our look at [Arc's shutdown and the move to Dia](/posts/arc-browser-shutting-down) covers what you can bring with you and the AI defaults in Dia, Arc's replacement.

## How to turn off Gemini in Gmail and Google Docs

One setting turns off Gemini in Gmail, Google Docs and the other Google Workspace apps for a personal Google account: **Smart features in Google Workspace**. On a computer, open Gmail, click **Settings** (the gear), then **See all settings**, scroll to **Google Workspace smart features** on the **General** tab, click **Manage Workspace smart feature settings**, turn the switch off, and click **Save**. The Gmail apps for Android and iPhone have the same switch under their own settings.

That switch also stops Gmail events, such as flight itineraries, from appearing in Google Calendar. Inbox tabs and Smart Compose are controlled by a different checkbox and stay on. Our guide to [turning off Gemini in Gmail](/posts/turn-off-gemini-in-gmail) lists what each of Gmail's three smart feature switches removes and how to hide only AI Inbox or Proofread instead.

## How to stop the Gemini app from using your other apps

The Gemini app can read your Gmail, Calendar, Photos and dozens of outside apps through Connected Apps, which is separate from Gmail's switch. Go to gemini.google.com, select **Customize** in the sidebar, open the **Connectors** tab, and choose **More options > Disconnect** next to each app you don't want Gemini to reach.

Disconnecting stops new access but doesn't delete past chats that used the app; you delete those separately in **Gemini Apps Activity**. To stop Gemini from saving new chats at all, turn off **Keep Activity** on that page, and our guide to [what changes when you turn off Keep Activity](/posts/turn-off-gemini-apps-activity) lists the features that go with it. Our look at [Gemini's new Customize menu for Connected Apps](/posts/gemini-connected-apps) sorts Google's list of 83 apps and shows what stays behind after you disconnect. If Gemini reaches your car, our guide to [Gemini on Android Auto with Personal Intelligence](/posts/gemini-android-auto-personal-intelligence) covers the same settings from the driver's seat.

If you use Gemini on the free plan, it now answers mostly with a lighter model, as our report on [the Gemini free plan's switch to Flash-Lite](/posts/gemini-free-flash-lite) explains. And Gems are being replaced by skills, which [our guide to moving Gemini Gems to skills](/posts/gemini-gems-to-skills) walks through.

## How to turn off AI Overviews and AI Mode in Google Search

You can't turn off AI Overviews in Google Search. Google's [AI Overviews help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683) says they are "a core Google Search feature, like knowledge panels. Features cannot be turned off." What you can do is select the **Web** filter after you search; Google says it "displays only text-based links without features like AI Overviews."

AI Mode is a separate, longer AI search experience that Google describes as an expansion of AI Overviews on its [AI Mode help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537). Neither help page lists a setting to turn AI Mode off. On mobile, Google says selecting **Show more** at the bottom of an AI Overview opens it, so skipping that button keeps you in the regular results.

You may also find a switch called **AI in Search** under **Labs**. Turning it off only takes you out of Google's early AI experiments and their email alerts, according to the same AI Overviews page. It doesn't remove AI Overviews from your results.

If you connected Gmail or Google Photos to AI Mode through Personal Intelligence, Google's October 16, 2026 privacy policy lets what Search learns from them shape ads too. Our article on [Google Personal Intelligence using Gmail and Photos for ads](/posts/google-gmail-photos-ads) shows how to disconnect them. Your phone number has its own ads setting in each Google account, which our guide to [removing a phone number from your Google account](/posts/remove-phone-number-from-google-account) shows how to turn off.

## How to turn off Copilot in Word, Excel and other Microsoft 365 apps

In Word on Windows, go to **File > Options > Copilot**, clear **Enable Copilot**, select **OK**, and restart Word. On a Mac, the same checkbox is under **Word > Preferences > Authoring and Proofing Tools > Copilot**. The checkbox works only in the app you change and only on that computer, so repeat it in Excel, PowerPoint and on your other devices.

Word for the web, iPhone, iPad and Android have no Copilot checkbox. There, the only way is a privacy setting that also turns off dictation, Translator and text predictions in every Microsoft 365 app on every device you sign in to. Our guide to [turning off Copilot in Word](/posts/turn-off-copilot-in-word) compares both methods device by device and covers work and school accounts.

## How to remove the Copilot app from Windows 11

On Windows 11, Copilot is an app you can uninstall. Microsoft says the [Copilot app](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/access-microsoft-365-copilot-on-windows) comes installed on new Windows 11 PCs and is pinned to the taskbar or Start menu, and its [IT guide to managing the Copilot app](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/manage-windows-copilot) says it can be removed from Settings:

1. Select **Start > Settings > Apps > Installed apps**.
2. Find **Copilot** in the list.
3. Select **More** (the three dots) next to it, then **Uninstall**.

These are the same steps Microsoft gives for [uninstalling any app in Windows](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/uninstall-or-remove-apps-and-programs-in-windows-4b55f974-2cc6-2d2b-d092-5905080eaf98). If you change your mind, you can get Copilot again from the Microsoft Store. Removing the Windows app doesn't touch Copilot inside Word, Excel or other Microsoft 365 apps, which have their own switch (see the section above). The Copilot app is only one of Windows 11's AI features; our guide to [turning off AI in Windows 11](/posts/turn-off-ai-in-windows-11) covers Recall, Click to Do, AI actions, Notepad and Paint as well.

## How to turn off Apple Intelligence on iPhone

On iPhone with iOS 27, you turn off Apple Intelligence one feature at a time in the **Settings** app. Apple says the new Apple Intelligence is "integrated deeply into the OS" and becomes available automatically on supported iPhones when you update to iOS 27 ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115)). Apple's iOS 27 guide to [turning off Apple Intelligence features](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/turn-restrict-access-apple-intelligence-iph3fed3f2c3/ios) lists a separate switch for each feature rather than one main switch:

| To turn off | In Settings, go to |
| --- | --- |
| Siri AI (Beta) | **Siri**, then **Turn Off Siri** (tap it twice to confirm) |
| Siri AI, but keep the older Siri | Turn off Siri as above, then **Turn on Siri > Siri Classic** |
| Message summaries | **Apps > Messages**, turn off **Summarize Messages** |
| Email summaries | **Apps > Mail**, turn off **Summarize Message Previews** |
| Notification summaries | **Notifications > Summarize Notifications**, turn it off |
| Priority notifications | **Notifications > Prioritize Notifications**, turn it off |
| Smart replies in Mail | **Apps > Mail**, turn off **Personalize Smart Replies** |
| Voicemail suggestions | **Apps > Phone**, turn off **Suggestions in Voicemail** |

![Menu paths to turn off Apple Intelligence features on an iPhone with iOS 27: Siri AI under Settings, Siri, Turn Off Siri; message and email summaries under Settings, Apps, Messages or Mail; notification summaries and priority notifications under Settings, Notifications; and Screen Time, Content and Privacy Restrictions, Siri to allow only Siri Classic](./images/turn-off-ai-features/iphone-ios-27-apple-intelligence-switches.webp)

To keep the AI version of Siri off for good, or on a child's iPhone, use Screen Time. Apple's [Screen Time guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/block-features-or-content-with-screen-time-iph3ff83f3b1/27/ios/27) says to go to **Settings > Screen Time > Content & Privacy Restrictions > Siri** and choose **Siri (Classic Version)** to allow Siri without AI, or **Don't Allow Siri** to block it. With Siri AI allowed, the same page lets you block single abilities such as **Image Creation**.

Apple Intelligence runs only on the iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 models and later, so older iPhones don't have these features to turn off. Our full guide to [turning off Apple Intelligence on iPhone](/posts/turn-off-apple-intelligence-iphone) adds the smaller switches in Mail, Phone and Journal, what has no switch at all, and the iPad and Mac paths. Since iOS 27.2 on October 26, 2026, Siri AI can also recall your past conversations and use your voicemails, and our [look at what iOS 27.2 changed for Siri](/posts/ios-27-2-siri-ai-memory) shows which settings limit that. To see or delete what Siri has already saved, our guide to [Siri history on iPhone](/posts/siri-history-on-iphone) covers the Siri app and the Siri & Dictation History button.

## What turning off AI doesn't do

Turning off an AI feature stops new use, but it doesn't delete what the AI already saved. Each company keeps that history in a separate place:

- **Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps:** delete past prompts in your Microsoft account's privacy dashboard, as our [Word guide's section on deleting Copilot history](/posts/turn-off-copilot-in-word#how-to-delete-what-copilot-already-saved) explains.
- **Gemini:** delete past chats, including ones that used Gmail or other connected apps, in [Gemini Apps Activity](https://myactivity.google.com/product/gemini).
- **Chrome:** turning off **On-device AI** is the one switch here that also deletes something, the AI model file Chrome downloaded.
- **Meta AI on Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp:** deleting the Meta AI chat only clears your inbox. Send **/reset-ai** in the chat first to delete Meta AI's copy, as our guide to [turning off Meta AI on Instagram](/posts/turn-off-meta-ai-instagram) explains.

Work and school accounts are another limit. In Microsoft 365 apps, the IT admin sets Copilot for you. In Gmail you can usually still turn off Gemini yourself, but the admin chooses the default and can turn Gemini on or off for each app on some business and school plans.

## How to check whether an image was made with AI

Turning off AI in your own apps doesn't help you spot AI-made images from others. For that, upload the original file to a Content Credentials viewer and to Google's SynthID Detector, then run a reverse image search. Our guide to [checking whether an image is AI-generated](/posts/check-if-image-is-ai-generated) explains what each result means and which everyday edits strip the labels.

## Which AI setting should you turn off first?

Start with the AI that can read your personal data, then deal with the buttons that only get in your way:

- **If you worry about privacy:** disconnect apps in Gemini's **Connectors** tab and turn off **Smart features in Google Workspace** in Gmail. These control what AI can read in your email and files.
- **If AI pop-ups slow you down at work:** clear **Enable Copilot** in Word and the other Microsoft 365 apps you use, and turn off **Help me write** in Chrome.
- **If you want a cleaner computer:** uninstall the Copilot app from Windows 11 and turn off **On-device AI** in Chrome to free the disk space its model uses.
- **If you're setting up an iPhone for a child:** use Screen Time to allow only Siri Classic, then turn off the summaries you don't want.
- **If AI answers in Google Search bother you:** use the **Web** filter. There's no setting that hides AI Overviews for good.
