Windows 11 has no single switch that turns off all of its AI. You turn it off one feature at a time in the **Settings** app: uninstall the Copilot app under **Settings > Apps > Installed apps**, turn off **Save snapshots** under **Settings > Privacy & security > Recall & snapshots**, set **Click to Do** to **Off** on the same **Privacy & security** page, and turn off AI actions under **Settings > Apps > Actions**. Many of these features exist only on Copilot+ PCs, so on an ordinary Windows 11 PC the list is shorter.

We read more than 20 Microsoft Support and Microsoft Learn pages on October 24, 2026, and checked them against each other. Every step below uses the Settings app or an app's own menu. We left out Group Policy, Registry Editor and the "debloat" scripts many guides suggest: Microsoft says its Turn off Windows Copilot policy is due to be retired, and the other AI policies are meant for IT admins managing work PCs. We didn't have a Windows 11 PC to test on, so the menu names come from Microsoft's own guides.

| AI feature | Where to turn it off | Fully off? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copilot app | **Settings > Apps > Installed apps**, then **Uninstall** | Yes, removed |
| Ask Copilot on the taskbar | **Settings > Personalization > Taskbar** | Yes |
| Copilot key | **Settings > Personalization > Text input > Customize Copilot key on keyboard** | Yes, set it to **Search** |
| Recall (Copilot+ PCs) | **Settings > Privacy & security > Recall & snapshots**, or remove it in **Turn Windows features on or off** | Yes, removable |
| Click to Do (Copilot+ PCs) | **Settings > Privacy & security > Click to Do** | Off, but can't be removed |
| AI actions in File Explorer | **Settings > Apps > Actions** | One action at a time |
| Notepad AI | Notepad's own settings | Yes, per Microsoft's Notepad guide |
| Paint AI | No setting | No |
| Downloaded AI models | **Settings > System > AI Components** | Yes, removed |

## Can you turn off all AI in Windows 11?

No, Windows 11 has no single setting that turns off every AI feature. Each feature is controlled where its other settings live: Recall and Click to Do under **Privacy & security**, the Copilot app under **Apps**, Notepad's AI inside Notepad. Turning off one doesn't change the others, and some features have no off switch for home users at all.

Microsoft's own pages also don't agree on everything. Its [Notepad guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-apps/enhance-your-writing-with-ai-in-notepad) says you can turn Notepad's AI off in Notepad's settings, while its [Microsoft 365 Copilot FAQ](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/frequently-asked-questions-about-copilot-in-microsoft-365-subscriptions) says "You can't turn off Copilot features in Designer, Notepad, or Paint." We note both answers in the steps below.

## Which AI features does your PC have?

Recall, Click to Do, the agent in Settings and Paint's Cocreator need a Copilot+ PC, so most Windows 11 PCs don't have them. A Copilot+ PC is a newer Windows 11 PC with an NPU, a chip for AI tasks, rated at 40 TOPS or more, plus at least 16 GB of memory and 256 GB of storage, according to Microsoft's requirements for [Recall](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/retrace-your-steps-with-recall) and [Click to Do](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/manage-click-to-do). If your PC isn't one, skip those sections.

Every Windows 11 PC can have the Copilot app and the Copilot key behavior. Notepad's Rewrite, Summarize and Write, and Paint's Image Creator, run as online services and need you to sign in with a Microsoft account and spend AI credits.

## How to remove the Copilot app from Windows 11

You can uninstall the Copilot app like any other app. Microsoft's [guide to managing the Copilot app](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/manage-windows-copilot) and its [app uninstall instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/uninstall-or-remove-apps-and-programs-in-windows-4b55f974-2cc6-2d2b-d092-5905080eaf98) give the same steps:

1. Open **Start > Settings > Apps > Installed apps**.
2. Find **Copilot** in the list.
3. Select **More** (the three dots) **> Uninstall**.

You can get the app back from the Microsoft Store later. Removing it doesn't touch Copilot inside Word, Excel and other Microsoft 365 apps, which have their own checkbox. Our guide to [turning off Copilot in Word](/posts/turn-off-copilot-in-word) covers that switch on Windows and Mac.

To keep the app but take the **Ask Copilot** button off the taskbar, go to **Settings > Personalization > Taskbar** and turn off **Ask Copilot**, according to Microsoft's [Copilot settings guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/windows/copilot/use-a-screen-reader-to-manage-copilot-settings-in-windows).

## How to change what the Copilot key does

You can make the Copilot key on your keyboard open Windows search instead of Copilot. Microsoft's [keyboard shortcuts page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/windows/keyboard-shortcuts-in-windows) gives this path:

1. Open **Settings > Personalization > Text input**.
2. Select **Customize Copilot key on keyboard**.
3. Choose **Search**.

Microsoft's screen reader guide lists the same option under **Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Keyboard** instead, and says the choices can include **Search**, **Microsoft Copilot** and **Custom**, depending on your account and installed apps. If you don't see it in one place, check the other. The same guide notes that if Ask Copilot is turned on in the taskbar settings, it overrides your choice for the key, so turn that off too.

## How to turn off Recall

Recall saves nothing until you turn it on, so on most PCs there is nothing to stop. Microsoft says saving snapshots, the screenshots Recall takes every few seconds, is off by default, and Windows asks your permission first ([Recall guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/retrace-your-steps-with-recall)). If you turned it on earlier:

1. Open **Settings > Privacy & security > Recall & snapshots**.
2. Turn off **Save snapshots**.
3. Confirm with Windows Hello (your face, fingerprint or PIN) when asked.

From the same page you can delete the snapshots already saved. To pause it temporarily instead, select the Recall icon in the system tray (next to the clock) and choose the pause option, according to Microsoft's [Recall privacy page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/privacy/privacy-and-control-over-your-recall-experience). **Advanced settings > Reset Recall** deletes every snapshot and resets Recall's settings, but snapshots you exported to a folder stay.

### How to remove Recall completely

You can uninstall Recall itself, because Microsoft treats it as an optional Windows feature:

1. Type **Turn Windows features on or off** in the search box on the taskbar and open it.
2. Clear the **Recall** checkbox.
3. Restart your PC.

Microsoft says any snapshots saved earlier are deleted when Recall is removed. To bring it back, check **Recall** in the same window and restart. On PCs managed by a work or school, Recall is removed by default until IT allows it, and Microsoft says an admin can't turn on snapshots without you.

## How to turn off Click to Do

Click to Do is on by default on Copilot+ PCs, and you can turn it off but not remove it. Click to Do is the tool that opens with **Windows key + Q** or **Windows key + click** and suggests actions for the text or image on your screen. According to Microsoft's [Click to Do guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/click-to-do-do-more-with-what-s-on-your-screen):

1. Open **Settings > Privacy & security > Click to Do**.
2. Set **Click to Do** to **Off**.

After that, the keyboard shortcuts and the right swipe on touch screens no longer open it. Other apps, such as Snipping Tool, may still show a Click to Do button, but Microsoft says selecting it gives a "Couldn't open Click to Do" error. Microsoft says Click to Do reads your screen on the PC itself and sends content online only when you pick an action such as **Search the web**.

## How to turn off AI actions in File Explorer

Microsoft points to **Settings > Apps > Actions** for turning off AI actions one by one. AI actions appear when you right-click a file in File Explorer and choose **AI actions**. Microsoft added them in the [September 29, 2025 update](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/servicing/os/windows-11/2025/09/september-29-2025-kb5065789-os-builds-26200-6725-and-26100-6725-preview): four actions for JPG and PNG images (Visual Search, Blur Background, Erase Objects and Remove Background) and Summarize for Microsoft 365 files in OneDrive. The same page says they aren't offered in the European Economic Area.

Microsoft's [Click to Do admin page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/manage-click-to-do) says users can disable the actions they don't want under **Settings > Apps > Actions**. The four image actions are the same ones Click to Do offers for pictures on your screen. Microsoft's pages don't say whether the **AI actions** entry itself leaves the right-click menu once every action is off. Microsoft's new right-click menu settings, rolling out since October 28, 2026, switch built-in commands on or off, but the release notes don't name AI actions ([what the customizable right-click menu changes](/posts/customize-right-click-menu-windows-11)).

## How to turn off AI in Notepad

Microsoft's Notepad guide says you can turn off Notepad's AI features, Rewrite, Summarize and Write, in Notepad's own settings. Open Notepad, go to its settings, and turn off the AI features option. Microsoft's guide doesn't give the exact name of the switch, and the same guide still describes the features as available to Windows Insiders, so your Notepad may not show it yet.

Microsoft's Microsoft 365 Copilot FAQ says the opposite: you "can't turn off Copilot features in Designer, Notepad, or Paint." If you don't find a switch, the features still do nothing unless you use them: they run only when you select Rewrite, Summarize or Write, and they need a Microsoft account sign-in and AI credits (60 a month with Microsoft 365 Personal or Family, as of October 2026). The FAQ suggests parents use Windows Family Safety to limit AI in Paint and Notepad.

## Can you turn off AI in Paint?

No, Microsoft offers home users no setting to turn off Paint's AI tools. Image Creator, which makes pictures from a text description, runs online and needs a Microsoft account and AI credits, according to Microsoft's [Image Creator guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-apps/use-image-creator-in-paint-to-generate-ai-art). Cocreator, generative fill, sticker generator and object select need a Copilot+ PC ([Paint's Copilot+ PC features](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-apps/use-copilot-pc-features-in-paint)). None of them runs unless you open it from Paint's toolbar.

Microsoft's [Windows AI policy list](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/client-management/mdm/policy-csp-windowsai) has separate switches for Cocreator, generative fill and Image Creator, but they are policies for IT admins on managed PCs. Microsoft also says images made with any AI feature in Paint carry a Content Credentials (C2PA) label; our guide to [checking whether an image is AI-generated](/posts/check-if-image-is-ai-generated) shows how to read it.

## How to remove AI models you don't use

You can delete downloaded AI models in **Settings > System > AI Components**. On a PC that isn't a Copilot+ PC, Windows downloads some AI models, such as its small language model, the first time an app asks for one, and Microsoft's [Windows AI documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/apis/) says these downloads "can be several GB." The same page says you can remove or reinstall them in **AI Components**. An app that needs a removed model may ask to download it again.

On a Copilot+ PC, the core models come built in. Microsoft lists them as [Windows Copilot+ AI components](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/topic/windows-copilot-ai-components-a9ef14e9-32a7-497f-b780-7b6fb63af793), part of Windows that updates through Windows Update, and its pages give no way to remove them.

The same **AI Components** page holds **Experimental agentic features**, which lets AI agents such as Copilot Actions work in a separate account on your PC. Microsoft's [agentic features page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/ai-features/experimental-agentic-features) says it is off by default and only an administrator can turn it on, so leave it off unless you chose to try those agents.

## What you can't turn off

A few Windows 11 AI features have no switch for home users:

- **Paint's AI tools**, as above.
- **The agent in Settings** on Copilot+ PCs, which answers plain-language searches in the Settings app with a small on-device model. Microsoft's [admin page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/configuration/settings/agent) says it only suggests settings and changes nothing unless you ask; only IT admins get a policy to turn it off.
- **Built-in AI components** on Copilot+ PCs, as above.

Edge has its own Copilot settings, and Chrome has its own AI features. In Edge, one switch under **Settings > Copilot and AI** hides Copilot from the toolbar and the right-click menu, and our test of [turning off Copilot in Microsoft Edge](/posts/turn-off-copilot-in-edge) shows what it leaves behind. Our guide to [turning off AI in Chrome](/posts/turn-off-ai-in-chrome) covers the browser, including deleting its roughly 4 GB on-device model.

## Which AI features should you turn off?

- **If you never use Copilot:** uninstall the Copilot app, turn off **Ask Copilot** on the taskbar and set the Copilot key to **Search**. This covers every Windows 11 PC.
- **If you have a Copilot+ PC and worry about privacy:** leave **Save snapshots** off, or remove Recall in **Turn Windows features on or off** so it can't be turned on by accident. Turning off Click to Do stops the screen-reading shortcut too.
- **If disk space is tight:** check **Settings > System > AI Components** for downloaded models you don't need.
- **If you share the PC with children:** Microsoft points to Family Safety for limiting AI in Paint and Notepad, since neither has a full off switch.

For Chrome, Gmail, Word, iPhone and Google Search, our overview of [how to turn off AI in each app](/posts/turn-off-ai-features) lists every switch in one table. If your PC runs Windows 11 Home or Pro version 24H2, which no longer gets security updates, see [what to do now that 24H2 support has ended](/posts/windows-11-24h2-end-of-support).
