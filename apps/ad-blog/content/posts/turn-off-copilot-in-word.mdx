To turn off Copilot in Word on Windows, go to **File > Options > Copilot**, clear **Enable Copilot**, select **OK**, and restart Word. On a Mac, open the **Word** menu, go to **Preferences > Authoring and Proofing Tools > Copilot**, and clear the same checkbox. That switch turns off Copilot only in Word and only on that computer, so Excel, PowerPoint and your other devices keep it until you change them too.

Word for the web, iPhone, iPad and Android have no Copilot switch at all. There, the only way is a privacy setting that also turns off dictation, Translator and text predictions, and that change follows your Microsoft account to every device. We read nine of Microsoft's support and Learn pages on October 23, 2026, and lined up which method works on which device and what each one takes away. We didn't have Word on our test computer, so the menu names below come from Microsoft's own instructions, not our screen.

## How to turn off Copilot in Word on Windows

The **Enable Copilot** checkbox in Word's options turns off Copilot in Word on Windows without touching any other feature, according to Microsoft's guide to [turning off Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/turn-off-copilot-in-microsoft-365-apps).

1. Open Word and select **File > Options**.
2. Select **Copilot** in the list on the left.
3. Clear the **Enable Copilot** checkbox.
4. Select **OK**, then close Word and open it again.

After the restart, Microsoft says the Copilot button on the ribbon is disabled and none of Copilot's drafting, rewriting or chat features work in Word. To bring Copilot back, repeat the steps and check the box again.

If you don't see **Copilot** under **Options**, your Word is older than version 2412. Check your version under **File > Account** (Microsoft's [version guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/lifecycle/lc-account/about-office-what-version-of-office-am-i-using) shows where), then update Word. As of October 23, 2026, the current release on Microsoft's [update history](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/officeupdates/update-history-microsoft365-apps-by-date) is version 2610, so an up-to-date copy has the checkbox.

## How to turn off Copilot in Word on a Mac

Word for Mac has the same **Enable Copilot** checkbox, inside Word's preferences, starting with version 16.93.

1. Open Word and select the **Word** menu at the top of the screen.
2. Select **Preferences**, then **Authoring and Proofing Tools > Copilot**.
3. Clear the **Enable Copilot** checkbox.
4. Quit Word and open it again.

To find your version, select **Word > About Word**. Microsoft's [Office for Mac update history](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/officeupdates/update-history-office-for-mac) lists 16.114.1 as the latest release on October 22, 2026, well past 16.93.

## Why Copilot is still on in Excel or on your other computer

The **Enable Copilot** checkbox only applies to one app on one device, so turning it off in Word leaves Copilot on in Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote, and on every other computer you use. Microsoft says each app has its own checkbox and you need to clear it in each app on each device.

That works in both directions. Turning Copilot off on a shared family computer turns it off for everyone who uses Word on that computer, not just for you.

Outlook works differently. It has a **Turn on Copilot** toggle instead of a checkbox (**Settings > Copilot** in Outlook on the web and the new Outlook for Windows, **Quick Settings > Copilot** on Mac, iPhone and Android), and Microsoft says that choice applies to Outlook on all your devices signed in with the same account. Classic Outlook for Windows doesn't have the toggle, and Microsoft gives no date for it. The Copilot app on Windows 11 is separate again; our [guide to turning off AI in Chrome, Gmail, Word and more](/posts/turn-off-ai-features) shows how to uninstall it.

## How to turn off Copilot in Word for the web, iPhone, iPad and Android

You can't turn off Copilot by itself in Word for the web or the Word apps for iPhone, iPad and Android; Microsoft says the only option there is to turn off **Experiences that analyze your content** in your privacy settings. Microsoft's [Copilot privacy page for home users](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/copilot-in-microsoft-365-apps-for-home-your-data-and-privacy) confirms that with this setting off, Copilot features stop working in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and OneNote.

Microsoft's guide to [finding your account privacy settings](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/access-your-account-privacy-settings) gives these paths:

- **Word for the web**: while editing a document, select **File > About > Privacy Settings**. In reading view, open the menu at the top of the page and select **Privacy Settings**.
- **iPhone or iPad**: in Word, go to **Settings > Privacy Settings**.
- **Android**: in Word, tap your account picture in the upper-left corner, then **Privacy Settings**.
- **Any browser**: sign in to [account.microsoft.com/privacy](https://account.microsoft.com/privacy) and go to **Privacy > Find privacy settings in Microsoft products > Microsoft 365**.

In those settings, turn off **Experiences that analyze your content**. On Windows and Mac the same option is called **Turn on experiences that analyze your content**, under **Connected experiences** (Windows: **File > Account > Account Privacy > Manage Settings**; Mac: **Word > Preferences > Personal Settings > Privacy > Manage Connected Experiences**), and you restart Word after you change it.

You only need to change it once. Microsoft says privacy settings travel with your account to your Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad and Android devices the next time you sign in to a Microsoft 365 app.

## What else turns off with the privacy setting?

The privacy setting turns off every Microsoft 365 feature that sends your document to Microsoft's servers for analysis, not just Copilot. Microsoft's Learn page on [connected experiences that analyze your content](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-apps/privacy/connected-experiences) lists them. The ones you'd notice in Word are:

- **Dictate**, which types what you say
- **Translator**
- **Text predictions**, which suggest the next words as you type (Microsoft says basic predictions keep working in Word for Mac)
- **Editor**'s online suggestions. Microsoft says Editor's basic checks keep working
- **Read Aloud** (when you're offline, it uses your device's built-in voice)
- **Automatic alt text** for pictures
- **Smart Lookup**, **Research**, **Learning Tools**, **Similarity checker** and **Transcribe**

In PowerPoint, Designer's slide suggestions go too, and in Outlook, suggested replies. If you dictate or translate often, use the **Enable Copilot** checkbox on your computer instead and accept that Copilot stays in the web and phone apps.

![List of Word features that stop working with the privacy setting: Copilot, Dictate, Translator and automatic alt text turn off; text predictions turn off except basic predictions in Word for Mac; Editor's advanced suggestions turn off but basic spelling and grammar stay; Read Aloud is listed and uses the device voice offline; Smart Lookup, Research and Learning Tools turn off](./images/turn-off-copilot-in-word/word-features-off-with-privacy-setting.webp)

Don't confuse this option with the one below it. Microsoft's [connected experiences overview](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/connected-experiences-in-microsoft-365) describes a separate switch that turns off all optional connected experiences, which also stops co-authoring and online file storage. You don't need that one to turn off Copilot.

## Can you turn off Copilot in Word with a work or school account?

No, the **Enable Copilot** checkbox and these steps apply only when you're signed in to Word with a personal Microsoft account, one you created with a personal email address. With a work or school account, Microsoft says the settings in its guide aren't available, and your organization decides whether Copilot appears and which privacy options you can change. Ask your IT department if you want it off.

## How to delete what Copilot already saved

Turning off Copilot stops new prompts but doesn't erase the ones you already sent. Microsoft keeps your Copilot prompts and responses as activity history, and its guide to [managing Copilot activity history](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/privacy/manage-your-copilot-activity-history-in-the-privacy-dashboard) explains how to remove them:

1. Sign in to [account.microsoft.com/privacy](https://account.microsoft.com/privacy) with your Microsoft account.
2. Go to **Privacy > Empower your productivity > Copilot > Your Copilot app activity history**.
3. Choose **Copilot in Microsoft 365 apps**.
4. Select **Delete all activity history**, then **Clear**. You can select an export option first if you want a copy.

Microsoft says prompts, responses and the files you work on with Copilot in its apps aren't used to train its foundation models.

## Can you just hide the Copilot button?

You can remove commands from Word's ribbon, but Microsoft says that only hides the button: Copilot stays on and you can still reach it from other places, such as the right-click menu. To change the ribbon, right-click an empty area of it and select **Customize the Ribbon**, as Microsoft's [ribbon guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/foundations-experiences/customize-the-ribbon-in-office) shows. Ribbon changes also apply only to the app you make them in.

## Which setting should you use?

- **You use Word on one Windows PC or Mac and want Copilot gone**: clear **Enable Copilot** in Word, then in Excel and PowerPoint if you want them clean too.
- **You also use Word on the web or your phone**: turn off **Experiences that analyze your content** once, and accept that dictation, Translator and text predictions go with it.
- **You only want fewer prompts on screen**: hide the button with **Customize the Ribbon**, knowing Copilot is still on.
- **Your Word is managed by work or school**: ask your IT department.

Google's AI works the same way in its own apps; our guide to [turning off Gemini in Gmail and Google Docs](/posts/turn-off-gemini-in-gmail) covers the one switch that controls it. For AI features in your browser, see [how to turn off AI in Chrome](/posts/turn-off-ai-in-chrome).

## FAQ

### Does turning off Copilot lower my Microsoft 365 price?

No. Both are settings inside the apps and your privacy settings, not in your subscription, so your plan and its price stay the same. If you're reviewing your plan, our look at [Microsoft 365 Family's move to 2 TB of shared storage](/posts/microsoft-365-family-storage-2tb) covers the other big change coming at renewal.

### Why don't I see Copilot in Word at all?

Microsoft's [Copilot subscription FAQ](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/Microsoft-365-Copilot/frequently-asked-questions-about-copilot-in-microsoft-365-subscriptions) says the AI features in Word on Microsoft 365 Family and Premium are only for the person who owns the subscription, not the people it's shared with, and that they're removed for users under a minimum age. The same FAQ lists Copilot as part of the Microsoft 365 Personal, Family and Premium plans, so it won't appear if you use Word from a one-time purchase instead; if that's Office 2021, see what [Office 2021's end of support](/posts/office-2021-end-of-support) means for you.
