You can still enroll a Windows 10 PC in Extended Security Updates (ESU). Microsoft's [consumer ESU page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/extended-security-updates) says enrollment is open "any time until the program ends on October 12, 2027," and it's free if you sync your PC settings to a Microsoft account. You start it from **Settings > Update & Security > Windows Update**, where an **Enroll now** link appears only when the PC meets every requirement. If the link is missing, one requirement isn't met yet, and the checklist below finds which one.

Your OS build number tells you whether ESU updates are actually arriving. As of November 25, 2026, an enrolled PC that has installed its updates is on build 19045.7846 or higher, from the November 10, 2026 security update. We checked every step, build number and rule on this page against Microsoft's own pages on November 25, 2026. We don't have a Windows 10 PC to enroll, so the steps come from Microsoft's instructions rather than our own screens.

## Is your PC already enrolled?

If Windows Update shows your ESU status and your OS build is 19045.7846 or higher, your PC is enrolled and getting updates. Microsoft's ESU page says you can "check your enrollment status anytime" in **Settings > Update & Security > Windows Update**, and if you bought ESU, the order also shows up in your Microsoft Store [order history](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/store/b/order-tracking). If you see an **Enroll now** link there instead, the PC is not enrolled.

The build number is the second check, because only enrolled PCs receive the monthly ESU updates. Microsoft lists each one as applying to "Windows 10 ESU."

1. Right-click **Start**, choose **Settings**, select **System**, then **About** ([Microsoft's instructions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/find-information-about-your-windows-device)).
2. Under **Windows specifications**, read the **Version** and **OS build**.

| OS build you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| 19045.7846 or higher | You have the November 10, 2026 security update ([KB5131096](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/5131096)). ESU is working. 19045.7850 is the November 13 fix that came after it. |
| 19045.6575 to 19045.7811 | You have some ESU updates but not the latest. Open Windows Update and select **Check for updates**. |
| 19045.6466 or lower | No ESU update has installed. The PC is most likely not enrolled, or enrollment didn't finish. |

![Chart of Windows 10 version 22H2 build numbers from October 2025 to November 2026: the last free update in October 2025 was build 19045.6456 and the enrollment fix for PCs that are not enrolled was 19045.6466, then 13 monthly ESU security updates raised the build from 19045.6575 in November 2025 to 19045.7846 on November 10, 2026, with shaded bands showing which build ranges mean not enrolled, behind, or up to date](./images/windows-10-esu-extended/windows-10-esu-build-check.webp)

Build numbers come from Microsoft's [Windows 10 release information](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/release-health/release-information) table as of November 25, 2026. It lists 13 monthly ESU security updates so far, from build 19045.6575 in November 2025 ([KB5068781](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/5068781)) to 19045.7846 in November 2026, plus a few out-of-band fixes such as 19045.7811 on October 26. The number goes up with every monthly update, so the newest build will be higher once the December update is out. If your build starts with 19044 instead of 19045, your PC is on version 21H2, which the consumer program doesn't cover.

## Why Enroll now is missing and how to fix it

The **Enroll now** link is missing because the PC fails at least one of Microsoft's requirements. Microsoft's ESU page says the link appears only "if your device meets the prerequisites," so work through these checks in order and look at Windows Update again after each one.

![Checklist of six things to check when the Windows 10 ESU Enroll now link is missing: version 22H2 in Settings, System, About; the latest updates plus the ESU preparation package KB5126256 and a restart; an administrator Microsoft account that is not a child account; no work or school management, domain, Entra join, MDM or kiosk mode; no existing ESU license on the PC; and, if the wizard fails partway, the KB5071959 fix and another try](./images/windows-10-esu-extended/enroll-now-missing-checklist.webp)

1. **Check your version.** Open **Settings > System > About**. ESU only works on Windows 10 version 22H2 in the Home, Pro, Pro Education or Pro for Workstations edition, so update to 22H2 first if the version says anything older.
2. **Install every update, then restart.** Microsoft requires the latest Windows update and the ESU Licensing Preparation Package, [KB5126256](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/5126256), released September 8, 2026. That package installs only after a security update from October 14, 2025 or later, such as KB5066791, and Microsoft says the PC restarts automatically afterward. If **View update history** lists "Extended Security Updates (ESU) Licensing Preparation Package for Windows 10 (KB5126256)," this step is done. The package prepares the PC but doesn't enroll it.
3. **Sign in with an administrator Microsoft account.** The account used to sign in must be an administrator, and it can't be a child account. If you normally use a local account, Windows asks you to sign in with a Microsoft account during enrollment, because the ESU license is tied to that account.
4. **On a child's PC, enroll from a parent account.** Microsoft's ESU page says the parent has to be a user on that PC, set up as an administrator, and then enroll from their own account.
5. **Make sure the PC isn't managed by work or school.** Enrollment isn't offered on PCs in kiosk mode, PCs joined to an Active Directory domain or Microsoft Entra, or PCs enrolled in a company's device management (MDM). A personal PC that is only "Microsoft Entra registered" can still use the consumer program.
6. **Check whether the PC already has an ESU license.** Microsoft also leaves out devices that already have one. Go back to [the enrollment check](#is-your-pc-already-enrolled) and look at your build.
7. **If the wizard starts but fails partway,** install [KB5071959](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/help/5071959) from Windows Update. Microsoft released it in November 2025 because "the enrollment wizard may fail during enrollment," and offers it to PCs that are not enrolled. Restart, then select **Enroll now** again.

If none of this works, contact [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/contactus).

## How to enroll in Windows 10 ESU

Once the **Enroll now** link appears, enrolling takes a few steps in Windows Update and covers the PC through October 12, 2027.

1. Open **Settings > Update & Security > Windows Update** and install everything offered. Restart if asked.
2. Select **Enroll now**.
3. If you're signed in with a local account, sign in with your Microsoft account when Windows asks. If you already back up your PC settings, Windows just asks you to confirm.
4. Otherwise, pick one of the three ways to enroll: start backing up your PC settings (free), redeem **1,000 Microsoft Rewards points**, or make a **one-time $30 purchase** plus tax.
5. Go back to Windows Update and select **Check for updates**. Microsoft says an enrolled PC receives all critical and important updates released after October 14, 2025, so a late enrollment catches up on the ones it missed.

One license covers **up to 10 PCs**. On each extra PC, open Windows Update and select **Enroll now**; if you're signed in with the same Microsoft account, select **Add device**. If another user on the same PC already enrolled it, you don't need to enroll again, because Microsoft's FAQ says every user on that PC is covered.

### Which option should you pick?

The free settings backup is the obvious choice if you're happy using a Microsoft account. The other two options cost points or money and differ mainly in what you keep.

| Option | Cost | What you give up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back up PC settings | Free | Your Windows settings sync to your Microsoft account |
| Rewards points | 1,000 points | Points you could spend on other rewards; Rewards orders are final |
| One-time purchase | $30 plus tax | Money; refunds follow Microsoft's digital purchase policy for your country |

![Comparison of the Windows 10 ESU enrollment options in the United States and in the European Economic Area: in the U.S. you can enroll free by syncing PC settings, with 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points, or with a one-time $30 purchase; in Europe enrollment is free as long as you keep signing in with the Microsoft account you enrolled with, updates stop within up to 60 days if you don't, and the $30 purchase lets you keep a local account; every option runs until October 12, 2027 and one license covers up to 10 PCs](./images/windows-10-esu-extended/esu-enrollment-options-us-europe.webp)

### Can you enroll with a local account?

Not completely. Every option requires signing in with a Microsoft account at least once, because the ESU license is tied to the account you enroll with. Microsoft's U.S. page doesn't say whether you can switch back to a local account after enrolling.

### If you live in Europe

The rules differ in the European Economic Area. On Microsoft's [Irish ESU page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-ie/windows/extended-security-updates), enrollment is free as long as you **stay signed in to the PC with the Microsoft account you enrolled with**. If you stop, "ESU updates will be discontinued for your device after a period of time, up to 60 days," and you have to enroll again. The $30 purchase "allows continued use of a local account (no sign in with a Microsoft account required other than to make the purchase)." The Irish page doesn't list Rewards points as an option, and the U.S. page doesn't mention the 60-day rule.

## Should you enroll or move to Windows 11?

Enroll now if your PC stays on Windows 10, because ESU is the only way a Windows 10 home PC gets security fixes, and Microsoft's ESU page lists no coverage after October 12, 2027. ESU covers security only. Microsoft's page says enrollment "does not provide other types of fixes, feature improvements, or product enhancements" and "does not come with technical support," so a Windows 10 PC won't get Windows 11 features such as the [customizable right-click menu](/posts/customize-right-click-menu-windows-11).

Enrolling doesn't block an upgrade. Microsoft's FAQ says you can still move to Windows 11 later if your PC meets the hardware requirements, so free enrollment makes sense even if you plan to upgrade. Windows 11 has its own deadlines. Microsoft's lifecycle page shows Home and Pro [version 24H2 ended on October 13, 2026](/posts/windows-11-24h2-end-of-support), and version 25H2 ends on the same day as ESU. Our [Microsoft end of support dates list](/posts/microsoft-end-of-support-dates) puts these Windows dates next to the Office ones, including [what changed for Office 2021](/posts/office-2021-end-of-support).

Windows updates don't cover your browser. If you use Chrome, [Chrome's new warning before insecure HTTP sites and its Enhanced protection setting](/posts/chrome-http-warning-default) are worth a two-minute check. If you use Edge, the browser that comes with Windows, our test of [turning off Copilot in Microsoft Edge](/posts/turn-off-copilot-in-edge) shows which switch hides its AI assistant. Other devices in your home have their own cutoffs: if someone relies on an LTE Pixel Watch 2 or 3 for emergency calls away from their phone, [Google's Safety Signal ends on December 12, 2026](/posts/pixel-watch-safety-signal-ending).

## FAQ

### Do I need to re-enroll in Windows 10 ESU for 2027?

No. Microsoft's ESU page says that if you are already enrolled, "your coverage will automatically continue through that date—no action needed." In Europe, keep signing in with the Microsoft account you enrolled with, or updates can stop after up to 60 days.

### Is Windows 10 ESU still free?

Yes, as of November 2026. The free route is backing up your PC settings to your Microsoft account (in Europe, staying signed in with it). The other routes are 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points or a one-time $30 purchase.

### Is it too late to enroll in Windows 10 ESU?

No. Enrollment stays open until the program ends on October 12, 2027. Microsoft warns that a PC is "more vulnerable and susceptible to viruses and malware before enrollment," so enroll as soon as the link appears.

### Does this apply to business PCs?

No. This is the consumer program for personal devices. Microsoft says a consumer enrollment is suspended if the PC later joins a domain, Entra or MDM, until it stops being used as a work device. Organizations use the separate commercial ESU program.
