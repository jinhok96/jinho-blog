About the transition from Gems to skills
Stay organized with collections
Save and categorize content based on your preferences.
Last updated: September 30, 2026
Google is introducing skills as the new method for custom AI instructions in
the Gemini app and Google Workspace. Skills provide a modular,
reusable way to apply tailored instructions to conversations and workflows.
Skills will replace Gems in 2027.
Skills offer several advantages over Gems:
Availability across surfaces:
Skills work in the Gemini app
and in Workspace apps, giving users a consistent instruction model across
chat and workflows. Gems can't be used in Workspace apps.
Use skills directly in chat:
Use a skill by typing
/
(soon to be
@
)
followed by the skill's name directly in any chat. Users don't need to open 
separate Gem windows for custom instructions.
Combine multiple skills:
Users can combine multiple skills in a single prompt.
They can also apply different skills across a conversation without copying
and pasting outputs.
Open standard:
Skills use the open, Markdown-based
SKILL.md
format.
This lets users create, customize, and transfer instructions across tools
without platform lock-in.
Availability and eligibility
Skills in the Gemini app:
Available to all Google Workspace
editions and users of all ages.
Skills in Workspace:
Available to users age 18 and older with Business
Starter, Standard, and Plus; Enterprise Standard, and Plus; Google
AI Pro for Education; or the AI Expanded Access add-on.
Transition timeline
For now, users can continue creating and using Gems in the Gemini app.
In Google Workspace Studio, users can no longer add the
Ask a Gem
step
to new flows, though existing flows with
Ask a Gem
steps continue to work
until 2027.
Over the coming months, Google will gradually phase out Gems across all
surfaces and automatically migrate remaining Gems in the Gemini app
to draft skills in the Gemini app. Currently, skills don't sync
between the Gemini app and Workspace apps. To use a skill across
both platforms, or to transition Gems used in Workspace, users must manually
recreate them in Google Workspace Studio.

What administrators need to do
To prepare your organization for the transition to skills, we recommend the
following actions:
Notify your users:
Inform users about the upcoming transition and key
dates based on your organization's edition (March 1, 2027 for business and
enterprise; June 1, 2027 for Google Workspace for Education) so they can
plan ahead for their workflows.
Encourage early adoption:
Make sure
skills are turned on
for your organization. Once skills start to roll out, direct users to
explore skills in the Gemini app and Workspace, and try combining
multiple skills in their day-to-day tasks. Share the
AI skills 101
handbook
and the Help Center articles on
creating and using skills in the
Gemini app
and
skills in Google Workspace Studio
.
Rebuild high-priority Gems:
Encourage creators to manually rebuild their
critical Gems as skills early. Share instructions on how to
manually
recreate Gems as
skills
.
Manually copy instructions and re-upload knowledge files to ensure prompt
verification and uninterrupted workflows before the automated migration.
Update Google Workspace Studio flows:
If your organization uses
automated Google Workspace Studio flows that include
Ask a Gem
steps, instruct flow owners to consult the
Workspace Studio transition
guide
to replace those steps with skills or alternative actions.

How automated migration will work
Note:
This section will be updated with full migration details at least 30
days before automated migration starts.
When automated migration begins, Google automatically converts existing Gems
into skills:
Draft status:
Migrated Gems are listed in the creator's skills list as
inactive drafts. Creators must review and activate each skill before they can
use or share it.
Preserved content:
Converted skills retain their original names,
descriptions, custom instructions, and uploaded knowledge files.
Sharing and permissions:
Drive permissions and sharing settings applied
to original Gems transfer to the corresponding skills.
